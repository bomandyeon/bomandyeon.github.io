Do you have the peach blossom mark?

Saju's "people look twice" star. Yes or no in five seconds.

How the check works

Four animals, four pointers

  • Monkey, Rat or Dragonpoints to the Rooster (酉)
  • Tiger, Horse or Dogpoints to the Rabbit (卯)
  • Snake, Rooster or Oxpoints to the Horse (午)
  • Pig, Rabbit or Goatpoints to the Rat (子)

We check from your year animal and from your day animal. If the animal it points to sits in another of your pillars, you have the mark. Solar terms set your year and month, so a January or early February birthday can belong to the previous year's animal.

Questions

FAQ

What is dohwasal?

Dohwasal (도화살, 桃花煞), the peach blossom mark, is a classic saju star for charm: the kind of pull that makes people look twice. Older books treated it as a warning about romance getting messy. Today it's mostly read as charisma.

How is it worked out?

From the animal (branch) of your year pillar and of your day pillar. Monkey, Rat or Dragon points to the Rooster. Tiger, Horse or Dog points to the Rabbit. Snake, Rooster or Ox points to the Horse. Pig, Rabbit or Goat points to the Rat. If that animal sits in one of your other pillars, you have the mark.

I don't have it. Is that bad?

No. Most charts don't have it. It's one particular kind of charm, the instant one. Plenty of people are magnetic in slower, steadier ways, and the rest of your chart says more about how you love than one star does.

Why does my birth time matter?

The hour pillar is the fourth place the peach blossom animal can sit. Without a birth time we check three pillars, so a no could turn into a yes once the hour is added.
