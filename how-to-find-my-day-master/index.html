How to find your Day Master

Short answer

To find your Day Master, look up the heavenly stem of your birth day in a Korean saju calendar (manseryeok), or enter your birth date in a free check that does the day count for you; that stem is your birthday type.

The manual way

Traditional readers use a manseryeok (만세력), a perpetual calendar that lists the stem and branch for every day. Find your birth date, read the day pillar, and the top character is your Day Master. For example, 庚 means you are Gyeong Metal, the Sword.

Three things trip people up. Use your local birth date, not UTC. If you were born between 11 pm and midnight, many Korean schools count you on the next day. And the day pillar does not care about solar terms or the lunar calendar; those affect the year and month pillars.

Why we say 'birthday type'

'Day Master' is a translation of 일간 (日干), 'day stem'. It is accurate but it sounds like a video game boss. We say birthday type because it tells you what it is: the type you get from your birthday. Every term like this is explained in plain English in the glossary.

FAQ

Is the Day Master the same as the day pillar?

No. The day pillar has two characters: the stem on top (your Day Master) and the branch below. There are ten Day Masters and sixty day pillars.

Can my Day Master change?

No. It comes from your birth date. Only the interpretation changes, depending on the rest of your chart.

What if I don't know my birth date for sure?

Your Day Master depends on the exact date, so an unsure date gives an unsure type. If you are choosing between two dates, check both and see which description rings truer.
