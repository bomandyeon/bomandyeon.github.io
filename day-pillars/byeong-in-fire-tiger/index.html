丙寅

Byeong-in: The Sun at Spring Dawn

The sun rising over a spring forest. Optimistic, generous, idealistic, and impossible to keep in a bad mood for long.

  • Yang Fire stem
  • Tiger branch · Wood
  • Pillar 3 of 60
  • Spouse palace: Indirect Resource 편인
LoveRed flagsWorkFAQ

Byeong-in puts 丙, the Sun, on 寅, the Tiger, the very first branch of spring. Inside the Tiger sit 甲 Wood, 丙 Fire and a little 戊 Earth, so this sun is rising on its own fuel. Picture the first light breaking over a pine forest in early February: cold air, a huge sky, and everything slowly turning gold. In saju terms, the Tiger is also where Fire is "born" (장생), so this is the sun at the beginning of its arc, full of promise.

You are an optimist with real stamina. You see potential in people, plans and messy first drafts, and you have the energy to back it up. The Di Tian Sui describes Byeong as 丙火猛烈 欺霜侮雪, fierce enough to scorn frost and snow, and you do have that bravery: you walk into cold rooms and warm them on purpose. The shadow is idealism without follow-through. A dawn is a promise, not a whole day, and Byeong-in can fall in love with beginnings and get bored by the middle part where the work lives.

BomYou make beginnings feel possible. Half the brave things your friends have done started with you saying, 'Why not?'

Byeong-in in love

Your spouse palace holds 甲 Wood, Indirect Resource (편인) for Byeong. Classically that is unconventional support: mentors, eccentric teachers, people who feed your mind. In love you are drawn to partners who are interesting more than conventional, the ones who introduce you to a new genre or a city you never considered.

You love openly and loudly. Grand gestures come naturally. You can, however, cast a partner as your muse and then lose interest when they turn out to be a regular person who also needs to do laundry. Stay for the second season. The slow episodes are where trust grows. Water types, calm and reflective, help you think before you leap and give your light somewhere to shine.

As a friend

You are the friend who hypes. You believe in people out loud, sometimes more than they believe in themselves. You invite everyone, you remember to include the new person, and you are excellent at turning a bad day into a story. You can overcommit and then have to cancel three things in one evening. Your friends forgive you, mostly because you apologize with snacks.

Red flags

  • You fall for potential and exit when it becomes a person.
  • You start projects at dawn and abandon them by lunch.
  • You hand out encouragement but never ask for any.
  • You overbook the week and then glow apologetically at everyone.

YeonDawn is great. So is 3 p.m. Try sticking around past the opening scene, Byeong-in. The middle is where the real story happens.

At work

Byeong-in is a natural starter and motivator: founder, teacher, recruiter, campaign lead, youth worker, creative director. You light up a team and make people want to try. Pair yourself with a finisher, ideally an Earth or Metal type who loves checklists. Your ideas plus their follow-through is a very good combination.

Byeong-in vs the other Byeong Fire pillars

Byeong-in is the most hopeful and idealistic of the six Byeong pillars. Byeong-o (丙午) is the sun at noon, much more intense and self-assured; you are the softer morning version. Byeong-ja (丙子) rises over winter water and is more disciplined and reserved. Byeong-sin (丙申), your clash pillar (寅申), shines on Metal and is practical and deal-minded. Byeong-sul (丙戌) and Byeong-jin (丙辰) have Earth in the spouse palace, which makes them more expressive and nurturing in love, while yours leans toward a partner who teaches and inspires you.

Chart notes

Chart notes for Byeong-in: the 寅 Tiger branch is Wood and hides 戊 (Eating God), 丙 (Friend), 甲 (Indirect Resource), read from a Byeong Fire point of view. Its main qi, 甲, puts Indirect Resource (편인 偏印) in the Byeong-in spouse palace. In branch relations, 寅 clashes with 申 (Monkey) and forms a six-harmony pair (육합) with 亥 (Pig), so a partner with a Monkey or Pig day branch will feel noticeably different to a Byeong-in person. 丙寅 is pillar 3 of the sixty in the 육십갑자 cycle.

FAQ

Why is Byeong-in described as a 'rising sun'?

The Tiger branch is the start of spring and the point where Fire is born in the twelve-stage cycle. The stem is the Sun. Put together, the image is a sunrise: early, bright and full of potential.

What kind of partner suits Byeong-in?

Someone intellectually interesting who can also handle the everyday. You crave novelty, but a calm, grounded partner keeps the relationship going after the honeymoon glow fades.

Does Byeong-in struggle with follow-through?

Many do, because the energy of this pillar is front-loaded. Building simple routines, or partnering with detail-minded people, helps your ideas actually reach the finish line.
