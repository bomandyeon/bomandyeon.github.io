Red flags, read by birthday type

Rain red flags: intuition without words

The Rain (Gye Water 癸): Intuitive, gentle and quietly perceptive. Senses what is wrong before anyone says it, and analyses text tone like a contract.

Short answer

The main Rain red flags are knowing something is wrong and saying nothing, overanalysing every message, and ghosting so gently it never quite ends; they come from sensitivity, not indifference.

Every birthday type has red flags, including yours. The useful question is which ones are habits you can talk about and which ones wear a relationship down. Yeon wrote the list. Bom made her add the green flags.

The red flags, one by one

🚩 Silent knowing

What it looks like: They sense the problem and write a poem instead of saying it.

What it usually means: Avoiding conflict. Encourage words over hints.

🚩 Text forensics

What it looks like: Every 'ok' gets analysed for hidden meaning.

What it usually means: Anxiety. Clear reassurance helps.

🚩 The soft fade

What it looks like: They drift away without a goodbye.

What it usually means: Kind intention, confusing result.

🚩 Hidden needs

What it looks like: They need reassurance but never ask.

What it usually means: Watch whether they can learn to ask directly.

Green flags that balance it

  • They notice what you feel before you say it.
  • They remember how you take your tea and what scared you as a child.
  • Their love feels safe, soft and deep.

BomBeing loved by a Rain feels like a cosy afternoon. Make sure they also feel safe enough to speak up.

The Water pattern

Water red flags are about flow: the Ocean's vanishing acts and endless 'undefined', the Rain's silent knowing and soft fades.

FAQ

Is a Rain a red flag partner?

No. Rain types are deeply caring and intuitive. Their risk is avoiding direct conversation, which gentle encouragement helps.

What is the biggest Rain red flag?

Knowing something is wrong and saying nothing. It lets problems grow quietly.

How do I date a Rain?

Be gentle, clear and consistent. Reassure them often and make it safe for them to speak openly.
